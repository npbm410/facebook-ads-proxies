## Start with the connection type, not the provider name

The word “proxy” covers several products with very different behavior. Buying the wrong type is an easy way to spend money solving a problem you did not actually have.

### Static ISP proxies

Static ISP proxies use IP addresses associated with internet service providers while being hosted on server infrastructure. The key operational feature is persistence: the assigned IP stays the same rather than changing continuously.

That can be useful for a permitted workflow that needs a consistent US connection across a work session. It also makes cost easier to forecast when the provider charges by IP rather than by traffic volume.

HypeProxies’ current public offering is centered on static ISP proxies. The provider lists US locations, HTTP support, unlimited bandwidth, unlimited threads, and 10 Gbps network infrastructure for its ISP plans.

### Rotating residential proxies

Rotating residential services commonly change the exit IP over time or between requests. That can make sense for approved, high-volume data work where rotating sessions are expected and the target permits it. It is usually a poor fit for a session that needs a stable network identity.

### Datacenter proxies

Datacenter proxies are usually fast and comparatively inexpensive, but their IP ranges are associated with hosting providers rather than consumer ISPs. They may be perfectly reasonable for permitted tasks such as internal monitoring or technical testing. For Facebook Ads access, the point is not whether they are “good” or “bad”; it is whether their network characteristics suit an authorized workflow.

If you need a persistent US-based connection and do not need a rotating pool, static ISP proxies are the more relevant category to compare.

HypeProxies’ public ISP comparison material lists HTTP support. If the tool you use requires SOCKS5 or UDP, confirm compatibility before ordering. Do not assume all proxy protocols are interchangeable.

A static ISP allocation is best evaluated for stability. The same IP can remain assigned to the same purpose rather than changing unpredictably. That is useful for a permitted work session where continuity matters.

A rotating residential pool is a different product for different workloads. HypeProxies does not currently show a live public price for residential proxies, so do not make a buying decision based on an assumed residential package.

## Why proxy quality alone cannot protect an ad account

This point deserves a direct answer because it is where many “Facebook proxy” articles drift into fantasy.

Meta reviews more than an IP address. Its Advertising Standards explain that review can involve ad creative, text, targeting, and associated landing pages. Meta can also review business accounts and assets, and ads may be reviewed again after they begin running.

That means a stable ISP proxy cannot fix:

- Ads that violate Meta’s advertising policies.
- Misleading landing pages or offers.
- Missing business verification where Meta requires it.
- Payment problems or suspicious billing activity.
- Unauthorized account access.
- Misuse of personal data.
- Prohibited targeting or discriminatory advertising.
- Attempts to use false identities or evade a restriction.

A good proxy can make a permitted network connection more consistent. It cannot turn a bad ad into a compliant one. It cannot make an account exempt from review. And it definitely cannot rescue a workflow that is prohibited by Meta or by the proxy provider’s own acceptable-use policy.
